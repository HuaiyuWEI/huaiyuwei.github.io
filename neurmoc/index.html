NeurMOC

Satellite-based monitoring of the ocean overturning circulation with deep learning

NeurMOC reconstructs Atlantic and Southern Ocean meridional overturning circulation (MOC) anomalies (relative to the 2004–2009 mean, the GRACE convention) in latitude–density space from satellite observations of ocean bottom pressure (GRACE/GRACE-FO), sea surface height, and 10-m zonal surface winds, using a simple neural network trained on the large ensemble of climate simulations from CSIRO's ACCESS-ESM1.5. This viewer presents the monthly reconstruction of MOC and its trend over April 2003 – December 2024 with uncertainty estimates.

How significance is assessed

The trend belongs to the default-product reconstruction. Its ±2σ uncertainty combines five one-sigma terms in quadrature: serially correlated residual variability from a 48-month circular moving-block bootstrap; mapping-error trend spread measured in held-out MRI windows; trend spread across trained network members; input-product trend spread; and GRACE measurement-noise trend spread propagated through the reconstruction with a 500-draw Monte Carlo experiment. Independence among these terms is not demonstrated. Hatching marks cells that fail the Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate gate intersected with the ±2σ rule.

What the curves show

The solid curve is the selected reconstruction. The shaded band and dashed trend are centered on the default reconstruction (GRACE JPL + DUACS + CCMP). The ±1σ band combines held-out-model mapping-error spread, network-ensemble spread, the time-dependent spread across the eight input-product combinations, and propagated GRACE measurement noise. When another combination is selected, its default counterpart is also drawn in gray. At 26.5°N the solid dark curve overlays the independent RAPID moored-array observations with their observational uncertainty band, shifted to the selected reconstruction's mean over the shared months — so the comparison is about variability only. The GRACE/GRACE-FO gap is shaded. The chip above the plot reads the trend against the local mean state: a positive trend on a negative (counterclockwise) cell means the overturning there is weakening.

About NeurMOC

The MOC moves heat, carbon, and nutrients around the globe, but direct measurements exist at only a few latitudes (e.g. the RAPID array at 26.5°N and the OSNAP array spanning 53°N to 60°N). NeurMOC closes this gap with neural networks that learn the relationship between sea-surface and sea-floor observables and the overturning circulation from simulations of a single climate model — the large initial-condition ensemble of ACCESS-ESM1.5 (CSIRO) — and then apply it to two decades of satellite observations.

  • Inputs — GRACE/GRACE-FO ocean bottom pressure, satellite-altimetry sea surface height, and 10-m zonal surface wind, all on the JPL mascon grid.
  • Output — the monthly overturning-streamfunction anomaly in latitude–density (σ₂) space for the Atlantic and Southern Ocean. The downloadable MAT/NetCDF files contain the default JPL + DUACS + CCMP reconstruction and its per-cell uncertainty; the viewer additionally carries the seven product-swap reconstructions.
  • Training — one climate model only: the ACCESS-ESM1.5 large ensemble is the only simulation set that archives every required field (ocean bottom pressure and density space verturning-streamfunction in particular) with a large ensemble. The cost of applying an ACCESS-trained network beyond its training model is measured on an independent model.
  • Validation — withheld ACCESS-ESM1.5 ensemble members, an independent climate model never seen in training (MRI-ESM2-0, from Japan's Meteorological Research Institute), the ECCO v4r3 reanalysis, and the RAPID moored array.

The reconstruction uses each dataset's own 2004–2009 reference-period convention; the orientation panel is the frozen ACCESS training-model baseline. RAPID instead uses its available April 2004–December 2009 mean and is mean-matched over shared months for the displayed comparison. The monthly uncertainty envelope combines an empirical mapping-error spread measured across 15 held-out MRI scenario-branch windows, network-ensemble spread, the month-specific spread across the eight input-product combinations, and GRACE measurement uncertainty propagated through the full reconstruction with 500 Monte Carlo draws. The downloadable files carry the same envelope. Where the held-out model does not provide complete support (mostly abyssal densities), the mapping-error term is taken from the nearest measured level above in the same latitude column. The GRACE term uses the JPL mascon uncertainty estimates and complements the product-swap term: one represents measurement noise and the other processing choices. It excludes GIA, geocenter, and C20–C30 correction errors, so it is not a complete estimate of every GRACE-related error source. Trend significance combines corresponding trend-space terms with serially correlated residual variability (moving-block bootstrap) and false-discovery-rate control.
