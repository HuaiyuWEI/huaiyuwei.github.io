NeurMOC

Satellite-based monitoring of the ocean overturning circulation with deep learning

NeurMOC reconstructs the Atlantic and Southern Ocean meridional overturning circulation (MOC) in latitude–density space from satellite observations of ocean bottom pressure (GRACE/GRACE-FO), sea surface height (altimetry), and surface winds, using neural networks trained on a large climate-model ensemble. This viewer presents the monthly reconstruction, its uncertainty, and the estimated trend over April 2003 – April 2024. The reconstruction retains interannual and longer-timescale variability.

About NeurMOC

The MOC moves heat, carbon, and nutrients around the globe, but direct measurements exist at only a few latitudes (e.g. the RAPID array at 26.5°N). NeurMOC closes this gap with neural networks that learn the relationship between sea-surface and sea-floor observables and the overturning circulation from a large ensemble of climate-model simulations, then apply it to two decades of satellite observations.

  • Inputs — GRACE/GRACE-FO ocean bottom pressure, satellite-altimetry sea surface height, and satellite surface winds, all on the JPL mascon grid.
  • Output — the monthly overturning streamfunction in latitude–density (σ₂) space for the Atlantic and Southern Ocean, with per-cell uncertainty estimates.
  • Validation — the approach is validated on withheld climate-model ensembles, on independent climate models, and against the RAPID moored array.

The uncertainty shown is the ensemble spread of the neural-network reconstruction accounting for observational noise. See the manuscript for the full method, validation, and caveats.
