How significance is assessed

Local checks whether a trend's ±2σ uncertainty interval excludes zero. Map-wide (FDR) adds a false discovery rate check for testing many locations at once. This extra check can reduce the area marked significant. Both rely on approximate uncertainty estimates.

What the curves show

The solid line shows changes over time; the dashed line summarizes the trend. Shading and the ± value give estimated uncertainty (±2σ). Color shows the direction of change, not its significance.

Series are centered to compare variability. In reverse-flow regions, the sign is flipped so a downward trend still means weakening. At the RAPID location, orange shows direct observations and their reported uncertainty. The vertical gray strip marks the gap between satellite missions.

Reading the maps and units

AMOC denotes Atlantic overturning; SMOC denotes Southern Ocean overturning. The vertical axis follows water density (σ&sub2;), with denser water toward the bottom. Transport is measured in Sverdrups: 1 Sv is one million cubic meters per second.

This reference is the ACCESS model's 2004–2009 mean. Red shows clockwise circulation; blue shows counterclockwise circulation.

About NeurMOC

The meridional overturning circulation (MOC) redistributes heat, carbon, and nutrients. Direct observations cover only a few Atlantic locations. NeurMOC offers a broader view of circulation changes across the Atlantic and Southern Oceans using ocean bottom pressure, sea surface height, and wind observations.

  • How it works: a neural network learns from ACCESS-ESM1.5 climate simulations, then estimates circulation changes from observed inputs.
  • How it is evaluated: tests in a second climate model, MRI-ESM2.0, and comparison with the RAPID observations at 26.5°N.
  • What you can download: the default JPL + DUACS + CCMP reconstruction and uncertainty in MATLAB or NetCDF format. This viewer also shows seven alternative input combinations.

NeurMOC is a satellite-informed, model-dependent estimate. Its uncertainty bands describe several known sources of error, but not every possible bias. Direct observational checks remain limited, particularly in the Southern Ocean and deep ocean.

Methods and interpretation

Monthly uncertainty combines model-transfer error, input-dataset differences, GRACE measurement uncertainty, and variation among trained networks. Trend uncertainty also accounts for estimating a trend from a short, autocorrelated record.

RAPID agreement was a secondary check during model selection; the default was not chosen to maximize it. Full methods, tests, and limitations are described in the accompanying manuscript.
